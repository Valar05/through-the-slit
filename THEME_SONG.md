# Through the Slit

Status: composed theme; audio performance and master not yet rendered or accepted.

## Composition

- **Form:** Great War procession hymn / industrial dark folk
- **Tempo:** 92 BPM, heavy 6/8
- **Key:** D minor, with the final chorus opening briefly into F before falling home
- **Lead:** weathered low baritone; controlled, close, never theatrical
- **Response:** mixed platoon choir, sung as people keeping time together rather than a polished trailer choir
- **Palette:** field drum, low brass, bowed cello, pump organ, chain and tendon percussion, one ruptured-bellows bass pulse
- **Shape:** spoken doctrine over a waking engine; restrained first verse; full procession on the first chorus; the bridge drops to feet, breath, and drum; final chorus adds the whole war party
- **Avoid:** glossy power metal, heroic major-key triumph, choir gibberish, constant wall-of-sound, modern electronic sheen

## Lyrics

### Intro — spoken

Never receive force you can redirect.  
Never redirect force you can evade.  
Never evade away from the objective.

### Verse I

They buried iron in the clay  
And taught the roots to pull.  
A hundred feet beneath the plates,  
One tendon carries all.  
The guns grew eyes, the hull grew scars,  
The crew became the nerve.  
We do not ask the road for leave.  
We teach the mud to serve.

### Pre-chorus

Turn the wound into a weapon.  
Turn the terror till it fits.  
Every acre has a memory.  
Every answer has a slit.

### Chorus

Through the slit — where the whole war narrows.  
Through the slit — smoke and shattered marrow.  
Hold the nerve. Let the root-feet grip.  
Turn the shell. Take the acre.  
Feed the breach with what it gives.  
The body breaks. The front persists.  
We drive the war through the slit.

### Verse II

Saint Mendel kept no holy book.  
He kept the things that lived.  
Each child received the parent’s wound,  
Then changed the shape it gave.  
If one hull dies, the acre learns.  
If one crew falls, we name it.  
The body is a temporary oath.  
The front is what sustains it.

### Pre-chorus II

Let the gun become an organ.  
Let the war party become nerve.  
What survives must bear the burden.  
What inherits learns to serve.

### Chorus

Through the slit — where the whole war narrows.  
Through the slit — smoke and shattered marrow.  
Hold the nerve. Let the root-feet grip.  
Turn the shell. Take the acre.  
Feed the breach with what it gives.  
The body breaks. The front persists.  
We drive the war through the slit.

### Bridge — call and response

**Lead:** What force can turn?  
**War party:** We do not take it.  
**Lead:** What threat can miss?  
**War party:** We do not face it.  
**Lead:** What ground lies forward?  
**War party:** We do not leave it.  
**All:** Plant. Pull. Rupture. Regrow.

### Final chorus

Through the slit — let the whole front answer.  
Through the slit — feet in blood and crater.  
Hold the nerve. Let the root-feet grip.  
Turn the shell. Take the acre.  
Give the next crew what the lost crew missed.  
The body breaks. The front persists.  
The dead still drive us through the slit.

### Outro — platoon, unaccompanied

The body breaks.  
The front persists.  
Through the slit.

## Generation / performance brief

Industrial Great War procession hymn at 92 BPM in heavy 6/8, D minor. Weathered low baritone lead with a mixed platoon response choir. Field drum, contrabass brass, bowed cello, pump organ, chain-and-tendon percussion, and a low bellows pulse. Begin with the three Tank Kata laws spoken over an engine waking. Verses intimate and grim; choruses widen into a march without becoming triumphant. Bridge strips to footfalls, breath, and drum before the final full war-party chorus. Organic, muddy, severe, memorable. No glossy power metal, no modern synth sheen, no trailer-choir gibberish.
